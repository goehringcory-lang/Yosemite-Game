# The 32px art pass

The world is drawn on a **32px tile grid** (it was 16px) in a 960x540 view,
with every sprite and tile mapped onto one **42-color Yosemite palette**.
Menus are still laid out on the 480x270 screen they were designed for;
`scripts/ui/ui_scale.gd` scales each menu layer 2x so their text and 32px
art keep the proportions they had.

## The palette (`tools/art32/palette.py`)

Ten ramps, dark to light: granite (6), warm tan (4), pine (4), meadow (4),
oak gold (4), bark (4), Merced blue/teal (6), golden-hour amber (4),
accents (lupine, shooting star, poppy, snow: 4) and night (2).
`python3 tools/art32/palette.py` writes `valley_days.gpl` (GIMP/Aseprite) and
`valley_days_palette.png`. The light is always from the upper left; a lit
edge steps one color up its ramp, a shaded one steps down.

## What changed

- `project.godot`: viewport 960x540, window 1920x1080, integer scaling,
  nearest filtering. Settings window sizes are steps of 960x540.
- `assets/tiles/world_tileset.tres`: `tile_size` 32; atlas cells, collision
  polygons, texture and y-sort origins doubled. Tile cells are unchanged, so
  every map keeps its layout.
- Scenes and resources: positions, offsets, collision sizes, radii and atlas
  regions doubled (`tools/art32/migrate_scenes.py`, applied once).
- Scripts: world-space pixel constants doubled (speeds, reach, particles,
  placeholder drawing, `RegionMap.TILE`).
- Art: every sprite sheet keeps its filename and layout, at 2x the pixels.

## Rebuilding the art (`tools/art32/build.py`)

The 16px originals stay in git (commit `293679d`); the build reads them from
there, so it is repeatable. Categories run in the order the art was upgraded:

| category | what | pass |
|---|---|---|
| `characters` | player, NPCs, critters | Scale2x smoothing, palette, one-pixel selective outline per frame |
| `terrain` | tile sheets, the painted ground maps | palette, then painted texture: blotches, flecks, grass tufts, water glints (tile sheets wrap seamlessly) |
| `props` | flora, plants, items, signs | Scale2x, palette, one-light form shading |
| `structures` | cabins, ranger buildings, fences, interiors | Scale2x, palette, one-light form shading |
| `maps` | park and area map pictures | palette with a light dither (relief data untouched) |
| `ui` | panels, portraits, gear icons, title painting | palette (title painting also 2x with a light dither) |

`python3 tools/art32/build.py [category]`. Lighting at runtime is the
existing day/night gradient (golden-hour amber, Merced-blue night), night
lights and cloud shadows, all on the 32px scale.

Run-time and code-drawn color is brought onto the palette too: the climbing
screen is painted in code, so `assets/shaders/palette_snap.gdshader`
(generated by `palette.py`) snaps everything on it to the palette, and
`tools/art32/snap_colors.py` snapped the color literals in the menu, climbing
and station code.

## Known limits

- The climbing screen is still painted at 480x270 density (palette-snapped, not redrawn at 32px).
- The pixel-art generators under `tools/` (npcgen, hubs, mapgen, plants, ui)
  still draw at the old 16px scale. Their output goes through `build.py`'s
  passes, not straight into the game.
  A hub builder also lays its scene out in 16px pixels:
  `tools/hubs/rebuild_village.sh` shows the whole round trip for Yosemite
  Village (build, `upgrade.py --fresh` on what it drew, then
  `migrate_built.py` on the scene it wrote).
- Detail is derived from the 16px drawings (smoothed, re-colored, shaded), not
  redrawn by hand, so shapes are the originals' shapes.
